# Presence

The green "online" dot looks trivial. At scale it's one of the noisiest features you can build: every user's status
changes all the time, and many people want to know about it.

## Detecting online and offline

Connections drop without warning (phones sleep, tunnels, crashes), so presence uses **heartbeats with expiry**:

```
client heartbeat every 20 s → SET presence:alice online EX 45
key expires (no heartbeat for 45 s) → alice is offline
```

- Online is confirmed by a heartbeat; offline is **inferred** from silence.
- Make the timeout a few heartbeat intervals long, so one lost packet doesn't flicker the status.
- An explicit disconnect (closing the app) can mark offline immediately.

## Multiple devices

Alice is on her phone and her laptop. She's online if **any** device is.

```
SADD presence:alice:devices phone laptop     (each with its own expiry key)
online = SCARD(...) > 0
```

## Fan-out is the real cost

When Alice goes online, who needs to know? Her 500 contacts, and maybe the 20 group chats she's in.

```
status changes/s × watchers per user = presence messages/s
1,000,000 changes/s × 100 watchers = 100,000,000 messages/s   ← too much
```

Ways to tame it:

| Technique | How |
|-----------|-----|
| **Only tell people who are looking** | Subscribe to presence for users visible on screen (the open chat, the visible contact list) |
| **Debounce** | Don't announce offline until it's been offline for 30–60 s; collapse online/offline flaps |
| **Batch** | Send "these 12 contacts changed" every few seconds instead of 12 messages |
| **Approximate for big groups** | "1,204 online" as a periodically sampled count, not per-person events |
| **Pull for cold data** | "Last seen 3 h ago" is fetched when you open a profile, not pushed |

## Storage

Presence is **ephemeral and high-churn**: keep it in memory (Redis or the gateways themselves), never in your main
database. "Last seen" can be written lazily (on disconnect, or batched every minute).

## Privacy

Many users don't want their presence shown. Make it a setting, respect it on the server, and don't leak it through
side channels (like delivery receipts).

## Try it

A game platform has 5M concurrent players, each with ~200 friends. Players change status (online, in-game, away) about
once every 2 minutes. Estimate naive fan-out and propose a design.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Status changes: 5M ÷ 120 s ≈ 42,000/s. Naive fan-out × 200 = **8.3M messages/s**. Design: subscribe only to friends
on the open friends list (often fewer than 20 visible), debounce away/offline by 30 s, batch updates every 5 s, and
keep presence in a sharded in-memory store keyed by user.
</details>

## Key takeaways

- Online = recent heartbeat; offline = expiry after silence.
- A user is online if any of their devices is.
- Fan-out is the cost: notify only watchers who are looking; debounce and batch.
- Keep presence in memory, never in the main database.
