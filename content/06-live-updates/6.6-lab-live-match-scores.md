# Lab: live match scores

A cricket final. **1 million fans** have the app open. Scores update about every 6 seconds; big moments (a wicket, a
six) bring a burst of commentary and reactions.

> **Interactive version:** [open the Live scores lab](#/labs/live-scores): it has the decisions as checked steps.

## Requirements

- Scores reach fans within **2 seconds**.
- Fans can post reactions, shown as aggregated counts ("🔥 12.4k").
- It must survive phones switching networks, and gateway deploys during the match.

## Step 1: Push or pull?

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Polling every 2 s = 500,000 req/s of mostly identical answers. **SSE** fits: one-way server → client, simple
reconnect with `Last-Event-ID`. (Reactions go up as normal POSTs.) A CDN-cached "current score" endpoint can serve
clients that can't hold a stream.
</details>

## Step 2: The connection tier

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

1M connections ÷ ~100k per gateway → **10–14 gateways** with headroom, least-connections balancing, 25 s heartbeats,
and an idle timeout above the heartbeat on the LB.
</details>

## Step 3: Getting each update to every fan

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

```
Scorer → Score service → store (match_id, seq, event)
                       → PUBLISH match:final {seq, score}
Gateways subscribed to match:final → write to their ~80k sockets each
```
One publish, one message per gateway, and each gateway fans out locally. Sequence numbers let clients resume after a
drop.
</details>

## Step 4: Reactions at scale

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

A six can trigger 300,000 reactions in 10 seconds. Don't broadcast each one: count in memory (sharded counters per
gateway, or Redis `INCR`) and publish an **aggregate every second** ("🔥 +48,211").
</details>

## Step 5: Deploys and network switches

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Drain gateways gradually; clients reconnect with backoff + jitter and resume from their last seq. Keep the last ~5
minutes of match events in a buffer for instant catch-up.
</details>
