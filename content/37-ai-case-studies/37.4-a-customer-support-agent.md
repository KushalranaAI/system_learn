# A customer support agent

Customer support is where AI agents first paid off at scale: high volume, many repeated questions, a knowledge base
that already exists, and a clear measure of success. It is also where they fail in public, because the agent speaks
for the company (lesson [37.7](#/lesson/37.7)).

## What the public numbers say

- **Klarna** announced in February 2024 that its AI assistant (built with OpenAI) had handled **2.3 million
  conversations** in its first month, **two-thirds** of its customer service chats, doing the work of about **700
  full-time agents**, in 35+ languages. It reported resolution time down from 11 minutes to under 2, a 25% drop in
  repeat inquiries, satisfaction on par with humans, and an estimated $40M profit improvement for 2024.
- In May 2025 Klarna's CEO told Bloomberg that focusing on cost had led to **lower quality**, and that the company
  would invest in human support again so customers can always reach a person.
- **Intercom's Fin** charges **$0.99 per resolution**, where a resolution is a conversation in which no further help
  is requested after Fin's last answer. Pricing by outcome makes "what counts as resolved" a business-critical
  definition.

The lesson for a designer: automation rate is easy to raise and easy to fake. Design for **resolution quality** and
an easy path to a human from day one.

## Architecture

```
customer ─► chat widget / email / voice ─► support API (auth: who is this customer?)
                                              │
                                   ┌──── triage / router (small model) ────┐
                                   ▼                                       ▼
                           support agent runtime                   human queue (helpdesk)
                           ├─ retrieval: help articles, policies (RAG, module 34)
                           ├─ tools (MCP / internal APIs), acting as this customer:
                           │    get_orders, track_shipment, update_address,
                           │    refund_order (≤ limit), cancel_subscription
                           ├─ guardrails: PII redaction, policy filter, groundedness
                           └─ escalate_to_human(summary, reason)  ──────────►  agent desk
                                              │
                         conversation store + traces + audit log ─► evals and analytics
```

Key integration points with existing systems:
- **Identity:** the agent acts with the logged-in customer's identity; tools only see that customer's data.
- **Helpdesk:** escalation creates a ticket in the same system humans use (Zendesk, Salesforce, Intercom), with the
  transcript and a summary, so the customer never repeats themselves.
- **Back-office APIs:** the same order, billing and shipping services the web app calls; their business rules apply.

## Retrieval over help articles

- Ingest help-centre articles, policies and macros with metadata (product, region, plan, last updated).
- Filter by the customer's **region and plan** before ranking: refund policy in Germany ≠ in the US.
- Answer **only** from retrieved content; if retrieval finds nothing good, say so and offer a human. An invented
  policy is the expensive failure ([lesson 37.7](#/lesson/37.7)).
- Gaps in the knowledge base show up as "no good retrieval" clusters: feed them to the content team.

## What the agent may do alone

Decide by **impact × reversibility × verifiability** ([lesson 35.7](#/lesson/35.7)):

| Action | Mode | Why |
|--------|------|-----|
| Answer from help articles | Alone | Grounded, low impact |
| Order status, tracking, invoices | Alone | Read-only, scoped to the customer |
| Update address before shipping | Alone, with confirmation | Reversible, customer-confirmed |
| Refund ≤ 50 EUR on a delivered, damaged order | Alone, within code-enforced rules | Small, rule-checkable, audited |
| Refund > 50 EUR, repeat refunders, goodwill credits | Human approves | Fraud and policy judgement |
| Account closure, legal or medical topics, complaints, threats | Human handles | High stakes, needs empathy and accountability |
| Promising anything not in policy | Never | The company may be bound by it |

Limits live in code: refund caps per order and per customer per month, rate limits per account, anomaly alerts.

## Escalation to humans

Escalate on: explicit request ("human please", always honoured), low retrieval confidence, repeated
misunderstanding (two failed turns), negative sentiment, sensitive topics, tool errors, or policy limits. Hand over
with a **summary, the customer's goal, what was tried** and the evidence, into the right queue. Measure time to a
human after escalation; an agent that makes humans harder to reach is a churn machine.

## Measuring resolution

| Metric | Definition | Watch out for |
|--------|------------|---------------|
| Automated resolution rate | Conversations closed without a human | Abandonment counted as "resolved" |
| **Verified resolution** | No re-contact within 7 days on the same issue | Slower to measure, more honest |
| CSAT / thumbs | Survey after chat | Low response rates, biased sample |
| Escalation rate and reasons | By intent | Too low can mean the bot blocks humans |
| Containment by intent | Per topic | Where to invest next |
| Cost per resolution | Tokens + tools + human time | Compare with human cost per ticket |
| Policy violations | From evals and review sampling | Must be ~0 |

## Try it

You run support for a subscription meal-kit company. Decide what the agent may do alone, and what needs a human.
Candidate actions: answer delivery questions, skip next week's box, change delivery address, pause subscription,
refund a missing ingredient (avg 4 EUR), refund a whole box (60 EUR), cancel subscription, answer allergy questions.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

- **Alone:** delivery questions (RAG + tracking tool), skip a week, pause, change address (if before the cut-off,
  confirmed by the customer), refund a missing ingredient (≤ 10 EUR, ≤ 3 per month, enforced in code).
- **Alone but careful: cancel subscription.** Blocking or delaying cancellation is a consumer-protection problem in
  many places; let the agent process it (after confirmation, one retention offer at most).
- **Human approval:** whole-box refunds (60 EUR) unless it's a known-carrier failure verified by the tracking API.
- **Human handles / no AI answer:** allergy and medical questions: give the ingredient list from the product
  database verbatim, plus a link to a human; never the model's opinion on safety.
- **Everywhere:** identity-scoped tools, audit log, "talk to a human" always available, verified-resolution metric.
</details>
