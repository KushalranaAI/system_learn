# Lab: go global

A SaaS product serves customers in India, Europe and the US from one region. It's growing, enterprise customers ask
for 99.99%, and European customers ask for EU data residency.

> **Interactive version:** [open the Go global lab](#/labs/go-global): remove single points of failure on the
> simulator, then make the multi-region decisions.

## Step 1: Within one region first

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Multi-zone: N+1 API servers with health checks across 3 zones; a DB replica in another zone with automatic failover;
replicated cache; staged deploys. Measure SLOs and error budgets before going multi-region.
</details>

## Step 2: The regional layout

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Three regions (Mumbai, Frankfurt, Virginia), each a full stack. **Each tenant is homed** in one region; its data and
writes live there (EU tenants in Frankfurt, which covers residency). Global data (logins, billing, tenant directory)
lives in a small multi-region consensus store.
</details>

## Step 3: Traffic

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

An anycast global LB routes users to the nearest region; requests for a tenant homed elsewhere are forwarded (or
served from read replicas for reads). Health-based failover with slow, gradual failback.
</details>

## Step 4: Region failure

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Each tenant's home region has an async standby in another region (in-continent for residency where required).
Failover means promoting the standby for that region's tenants (RPO = seconds of lag, RTO = minutes), with capacity
reserved in the target region. Rehearse it quarterly with a game day.
</details>
