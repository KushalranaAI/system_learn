# Lab: a gym class booking system

Use everything from this module: clarify, write requirements, estimate, sketch,
and record tradeoffs.

## The brief

> *"I run three gyms. Members book yoga, spin and HIIT classes on WhatsApp
> and it's chaos. Classes overbook, people don't show up, and my staff spend
> hours on it. I want an app."*
> **Priya, gym owner**

## Step 1: Interview the stakeholder (10 min)

Write the questions you'd ask Priya. Then open the answers.

<details>
<summary>Priya's answers</summary>

- **Members:** about 4,000 across three gyms; ~1,500 book at least weekly.
- **Classes:** ~60 a day in total, 15–30 spots each.
- **Booking opens** 7 days ahead at 8:00 pm. Popular spin classes fill in
  under 2 minutes.
- **Overbooking** is the #1 complaint. "Two people can't get the same last bike."
- **No-shows:** she wants a waitlist. If someone cancels, the next person gets
  the spot and a notification.
- **Cancellation:** free until 2 hours before; after that it counts as a no-show.
- **Staff** need to see today's attendance list and mark check-ins.
- **Payments** are handled by monthly memberships already, so out of scope.
- **Budget:** "Not a lot. It's a small business."
</details>

## Step 2: Write the requirements (5 min)

Write functional, non-functional and out-of-scope lists.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

**Functional**
1. Members browse classes for the next 7 days.
2. Members book a spot; if the class is full they join the waitlist.
3. Members cancel; the first waitlisted member is promoted and notified.
4. Staff see the attendance list and mark check-ins.
5. Late cancels and no-shows are recorded.

**Non-functional**
- **Correctness under contention:** never more bookings than spots, even when
  hundreds of people tap "Book" at 8:00:00 pm.
- **Burst traffic:** handle the 8 pm spike (see estimates).
- **Latency:** booking answers in < 1 s, even at the spike.
- **Availability:** 99.5% is fine. An hour of downtime at 3 am is OK, at 8 pm
  it is not.
- **Cost:** small. A single region and a single database.

**Out of scope:** payments, trainer payroll, video classes.
</details>

## Step 3: Estimate (5 min)

How many requests per second at **8:00 pm** on booking night?

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Say 800 members try to book within the first 60 seconds, and each refreshes the
schedule ~5 times and taps book ~2 times (retries included).
800 × 7 actions ÷ 60 s ≈ **~100 req/s for one minute**; near zero the rest of
the day.
Storage: 60 classes × 30 bookings × 365 ≈ 650k rows a year, which is tiny.

**Conclusion:** the scale is small. The hard part is **correctness** (no double
booking), not throughput.
</details>

## Step 4: Sketch v1 (5 min)

Draw the components, the API and the tables.

<details>
<summary>Reference design</summary>

```
Mobile app ─▶ API server ─▶ Postgres
                  │
                  └─▶ Push / SMS provider (waitlist notifications)
```

**API**
```
GET    /classes?from=&to=          list classes with spots left
POST   /classes/:id/bookings       book (or join waitlist)  → 201 | 409 full
DELETE /bookings/:id               cancel
GET    /classes/:id/attendance     staff only
POST   /bookings/:id/check-in      staff only
```

**Tables**
```
members(id, name, phone, gym_id)
classes(id, gym_id, type, starts_at, capacity)
bookings(id, class_id, member_id, status, created_at)
    status ∈ booked | waitlisted | cancelled | late_cancel | attended | no_show
    UNIQUE (class_id, member_id)
```

**No double booking:** in one transaction, lock the class row
(`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`), count `booked` rows, then insert as `booked` if the
count is below capacity, or as `waitlisted` otherwise. The lock serialises
bookings *per class*, which is cheap at this scale and always correct.
</details>

## Step 5: Record two tradeoffs (5 min)

Write two short ADRs. Ideas: row lock vs. optimistic concurrency; one server vs.
two for availability at 8 pm; push notifications vs. SMS.

<details>
<summary>Example</summary>

**ADR-001: Lock the class row when booking.**
+ Simple and provably correct; contention is limited to one class.
− Bookings for one popular class queue behind each other. At ~100 req/s that's
  milliseconds. Revisit only if a single class gets thousands of bookings per
  second.

**ADR-002: One API server + managed Postgres, plus a second server only on
booking nights.**
+ Cheap.
− A single point of failure most of the day. Accepted, given the 99.5% target
  and that the critical window is short and predictable.
</details>
