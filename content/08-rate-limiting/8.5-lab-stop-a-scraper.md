# Lab: stop a scraper

A product-listing API serves 10,000 real requests a second. A scraper starts hitting random product pages at
**50,000 req/s** from a handful of IPs. Random pages miss the cache, so the database starts to drown.

> **Interactive version:** [open the Stop a scraper lab](#/labs/stop-a-scraper) and tune a rate limiter on the
> simulator.

## Step 1: Which key?

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Not per path (the scraper never repeats a path), and not global (that 429s real users too). **Per IP** at the edge:
the scraper uses a few IPs, each sending thousands of req/s.
</details>

## Step 2: Which limit?

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Real users send ~2 req/s; a busy office or carrier NAT sends ~40 req/s from one IP. The scraper sends ~12,500 req/s
per IP. Anything from about **50 to a few hundred req/s per IP** blocks >99% of the scraper and no real users. Below
40, offices start getting 429s.
</details>

## Step 3: Where?

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

At the edge, **in front of the load balancer**, so blocked requests never consume server, cache or database capacity.
Return 429 with Retry-After.
</details>

## Reflect

- The scraper switches to 5,000 residential IPs at 10 req/s each. What now?
- Which endpoints deserve their own, stricter limits?
