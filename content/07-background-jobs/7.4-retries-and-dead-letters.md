# Retries and dead letters

Jobs fail. Most failures are temporary: a timeout, a deploy, a rate limit. Some are permanent: a corrupted file, a
deleted user, a bug. Good retry handling heals the first kind and quarantines the second.

## Classify the error

| Error | Retry? |
|-------|--------|
| Timeout, connection reset, 503, 429 | ✅ yes, with backoff |
| Deadlock / serialisation failure | ✅ yes, soon |
| 400 bad input, validation failure | ❌ it will fail every time |
| 404: the thing no longer exists | ❌ usually mark it done or skipped |
| Bug in the worker (exception) | ⚠️ a few times, then dead-letter for a human |

## Exponential backoff with jitter

```
delay = min(max_delay, base × 2^attempt) × random(0.5, 1.5)
attempt 1 → ~1 s, 2 → ~2 s, 3 → ~4 s, 4 → ~8 s … capped at 10 min
```

- **Exponential:** gives a struggling dependency time to recover instead of hammering it.
- **Jitter:** spreads retries out, so 50,000 failed jobs don't all retry at the same instant (a retry storm).
- **Cap** the delay and the **number of attempts** (e.g. 8–10).

## Dead-letter queues (DLQ)

After the last attempt, move the job to a **dead-letter queue** instead of retrying forever or dropping it.

```
main queue ──fail×N──▶ DLQ ──▶ alert + dashboard ──▶ human fixes the cause ──▶ redrive back to the main queue
```

- Every DLQ needs **an alert** and **an owner**. A DLQ nobody looks at is a black hole.
- Keep the error, stack trace, attempt count and timestamps with the job.
- After fixing the bug, **redrive** dead jobs back to the main queue (they must be idempotent, lesson 7.3).

## Poison messages

A **poison message** crashes the worker every time it's processed. Without limits, it gets redelivered forever and
blocks a worker slot (or a whole ordered partition). Max attempts plus a DLQ handles it automatically.

## Protect the dependencies

Retries multiply load. If a downstream service is down and 10,000 jobs each retry 8 times, you send it 80,000 extra
calls just as it recovers.
- Use a **circuit breaker** (Module 12): after many failures, stop calling for a while and fail fast.
- Pause the queue's consumers during known outages.
- Use **retry budgets**: retries may be at most ~10% of normal traffic.

## Try it

An SMS job calls a provider that returns `429 Too Many Requests` with `Retry-After: 30`. Your worker retries
immediately, up to 3 times, then drops the job. What goes wrong, and what's better?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Immediate retries all hit the same rate limit and burn the 3 attempts in a second, then the SMS is silently lost.
Better: honour `Retry-After` (wait ≥ 30 s), use exponential backoff with jitter and more attempts, throttle the
workers' send rate below the provider's limit, and dead-letter (with an alert) instead of dropping.
</details>

## Key takeaways

- Retry temporary errors; don't retry permanent ones.
- Use exponential backoff with jitter, capped in delay and attempts.
- Send exhausted jobs to a dead-letter queue with an alert and an owner, then redrive after fixing.
- Retries add load: protect dependencies with circuit breakers and retry budgets.
