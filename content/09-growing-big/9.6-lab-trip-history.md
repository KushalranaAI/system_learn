# Lab: a ride-hailing trip history

A ride app records every trip event (requested, accepted, started, location checkpoints, ended, rated):
**20,000 writes a second** at peak and 5,000 reads (riders and drivers opening trip history). Years of trips add up to
billions of rows.

> **Interactive version:** [open the Trip history lab](#/labs/trip-history) to shard the database on the simulator.

## Step 1: Why replicas won't help

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

This is **write-heavy**. Replicas replay every write, so they add read capacity, not write capacity. The biggest
single database (~11,000 writes/s) can't take 20,000.
</details>

## Step 2: Shard key

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Shard by **`rider_id`** for trip history: "my trips" hits one shard. Drivers also need "my trips", so either keep a
second copy keyed by `driver_id` (written asynchronously), or a driver-trips index table. Avoid `trip_id` hashing,
which would scatter "my trips" across all shards, and avoid time ranges, which make the newest shard hot.
</details>

## Step 3: Hot and cold

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Within each shard, **range-partition by month**. Keep 12 months hot; archive older partitions to object storage as
Parquet; drop raw location checkpoints after 90 days and keep a simplified route.
</details>

## Step 4: Growth

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Use 1,024 logical shards mapped onto 5 physical shards today. When writes grow, move logical shards to new servers
(copy → catch up → cut over), with no re-hashing.
</details>
