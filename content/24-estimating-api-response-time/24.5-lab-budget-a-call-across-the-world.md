# Lab: budget a call across the world

A travel booking API runs in one region. Users in Australia, about 10,000 km away, wait over a second for their first
search. Estimate where the time goes, then cut it.

> **Interactive version:** [open the latency budget lab](#/labs/api-latency-budget) to work through it in the latency
> simulation, with checked goals.

## Step 1: Estimate one round trip

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

2 × 10,000 km × 1.6 ÷ 200 km/ms = 160 ms, plus ~20 ms last mile: **~180 ms**.
</details>

## Step 2: The first call (goal ≤ 450 ms)

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

The starting design pays DNS, TCP + TLS 1.2 (3 round trips = 540 ms), one round trip for the request, ~4 slow-start
round trips for a 120 KB response, and server work: ~1.45 s. Terminating at an edge near the user makes handshakes
short local trips, and HTTP/3 needs one combined handshake: ~420 ms.
</details>

## Step 3: Every call after (goal ≤ 230 ms)

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

On a warm connection, the request still crosses the ocean (~180 ms). What's left to cut: server work (6 sequential
queries → 2, or run them in parallel) and the download (return 40 KB instead of 120 KB). That gets to ~217 ms.
</details>

## Step 4: Under 100 ms?

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

Not from that region. Serve Australian users from a region or edge cache in or near Australia: search results for
popular routes can be cached at the edge, and a regional read replica of the inventory can serve the rest.
</details>
