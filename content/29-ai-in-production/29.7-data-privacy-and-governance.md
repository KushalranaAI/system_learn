# Data, privacy and governance

An AI feature moves user data to new places: prompts sent to a model provider, conversations stored for debugging,
traces with full prompt content, embeddings in a vector DB, long-term "memories", eval sets built from real tickets,
and maybe training data. Each copy is a privacy, security and legal question. Governance is answering those questions
**before** launch and being able to prove it later.

## Map the data flow first

```
user ─► app ─► guardrails (redaction) ─► model provider (region? retention? training?)
          │                                     │
          ├─► conversation store (how long?)    └─► provider abuse logs (vendor policy)
          ├─► traces with prompt content (who can see? how long?)
          ├─► vector DB: embeddings of docs and memories (deletable per user?)
          ├─► eval / golden sets (anonymised?)
          └─► fine-tuning datasets (consent?)
```

For each box: what data, legal basis, region, retention, who has access, how it's deleted.

## Training on user data

The biggest question users ask: "will my data train your model?"

- **Model providers' APIs:** OpenAI states API data is not used for training by default (since March 2023) and is
  kept up to 30 days in abuse-monitoring logs, with zero-data-retention available to approved customers. Anthropic
  likewise does not train on commercial API data by default. Read the current terms for your provider and plan; put
  them in your vendor register.
- **Consumer products differ:** consumer chat apps often train on conversations unless the user opts out. This is
  why companies ban or restrict employee use of consumer AI tools. Samsung restricted generative AI tools in 2023
  after engineers pasted source code into ChatGPT ([30.7](#/lesson/30.7)).
- **Your own training/fine-tuning** on customer data needs a legal basis (consent or contract terms), opt-outs,
  de-identification, and a way to honour deletion: once data is in model weights you can't delete one person, so
  keep raw training data out of weights where possible, and prefer retrieval (deletable) for customer-specific
  knowledge.

## Retention

| Data | Suggested retention | Why |
|------|---------------------|-----|
| Conversation for the user's own history | Until the user deletes it | Product feature |
| Full prompt/response traces | 7-30 days, restricted access | Debugging; high sensitivity |
| Trace metadata (tokens, latency, ids) | 13 months | Cost and trend analysis |
| Audit log of agent actions | Per legal/financial rules (often years) | Accountability |
| Eval cases from real traffic | Until obsolete, anonymised | Regression testing |
| Long-term memories | Until user deletes, with expiry | User control |

Deletion must reach **every copy**: DB rows, vector embeddings, caches (semantic caches store answers), traces,
backups by policy, and data at the provider if retained.

## Data residency

Regulations and customer contracts may require data (prompts included) to stay in a region (e.g. EU). Choose
provider regions or deployments accordingly, and check **every** hop: the model, the vector DB, the tracing vendor,
the fallback provider in your failover chain ([lesson 29.6](#/lesson/29.6)). A failover to a US endpoint can break an EU residency
promise.

## Access and multi-tenancy

- Retrieval and memory filter by tenant and user **before** similarity search ([lesson 29.4](#/lesson/29.4), LLM08).
- Engineers viewing prompts in traces is access to customer data: role-based access, logged, time-limited.
- Separate indexes or namespaces per tenant for strict customers.

## Audit and model cards

**Audit:** be able to reconstruct any AI decision: input, retrieved context, model and prompt version, tools called,
output, who approved. This is the trace + audit log from [lessons 29.2](#/lesson/29.2) and [28.7](#/lesson/28.7), retained per policy.

**Model cards** (Mitchell et al., 2019) document a model or AI system: intended use, out-of-scope uses, training
data, evaluation results by subgroup, known limitations. Write a short one for each AI **feature** too: which models,
what data it sees, eval results, failure modes, human oversight, owner. It is the place a reviewer, auditor or new
team member starts.

## Regulation, briefly

- **GDPR** (and similar laws): legal basis, data minimisation, user rights (access, deletion), DPIAs for high-risk
  processing, data processing agreements with model providers.
- **EU AI Act:** obligations for general-purpose AI model providers apply from August 2025; obligations for
  "high-risk" uses (hiring, credit, essential services) phase in later; a 2026 agreement moved those dates to late
  2027-2028. Check whether your use case is high-risk; most support bots are not, but must disclose that users are
  talking to an AI.

## Try it

Write a data policy (one page) for an AI support assistant used by EU and US customers.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

- **Data sent to models:** ticket text and help articles, with PII redacted (cards, IDs); provider under a DPA, no
  training on our data, EU traffic to EU-region deployments; fallback providers meet the same terms.
- **Training:** we don't fine-tune on customer conversations without opt-in; improvements come from prompts,
  retrieval and anonymised eval sets.
- **Retention:** conversations 12 months then deleted; full traces 14 days, support-engineering role only, access
  logged; metrics 13 months; agent action audit log 7 years (refunds are financial records).
- **User rights:** deletion removes conversations, memories, embeddings and cached answers within 30 days.
- **Transparency:** users are told they're talking to an AI and can always reach a human.
- **Governance:** feature card with models, data, eval results and owner; reviewed at each model change.
</details>
