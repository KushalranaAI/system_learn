# Lab: a fast product catalogue

An online store's catalogue gets **20,000 product reads a second** at peak, and a few hundred price and stock updates.
The database tops out around 15,000 reads a second.

> **Interactive version:** [open the Product catalogue lab](#/labs/product-catalogue) and build it on the simulator.
> For the edge (CDN) version of the same idea, try [Serve a photo feed at the edge](#/labs/photo-feed).

## Step 1: Choose the cache pattern

Products are read constantly and edited rarely; a price change should show within a minute.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

**Cache-aside** in Redis:
- key `product:v1:{id}`, TTL 10 minutes ± jitter;
- on edit: update the DB, then `DEL product:v1:{id}`;
- the checkout reads price and stock from the database inside the order transaction, never from the cache.
</details>

## Step 2: Add the cache

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

```
Client ─▶ LB ─▶ API ×2 ─┬─▶ Redis        (80–90% of reads end here)
                         └─▶ Postgres     (misses + all writes)
```
At an 80% hit ratio, Postgres sees 4,000 reads/s instead of 20,000: about 30% busy instead of overloaded. The p99
falls from seconds to tens of milliseconds.
</details>

## Step 3: Survive a cache restart

Redis restarts and comes back empty. For a while, every read is a miss.

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

With the cache empty, all 20,000 reads/s fall on Postgres, so it must survive the warm-up:
- give the database headroom for the miss rate (a bigger instance or a read replica wired to every server);
- coalesce misses per key so one hot product isn't loaded 1,000 times at once;
- warm the top products right after a restart, before taking traffic.
</details>
