# Fan-out patterns

One event, many recipients. A user posts and 10,000 followers' feeds need it. A price changes and 12 services care.
How you fan out decides cost, latency and what breaks under celebrities.

## Pub/sub fan-out between services

```
OrderPlaced ──▶ topic "orders" ──▶ billing (group)
                               ├──▶ shipping (group)
                               ├──▶ email (group)
                               └──▶ analytics (group)
```

Each service subscribes independently. Adding a consumer doesn't change the producer. Keep events **self-contained
enough** (ids plus the key fields) so consumers don't all call back to the producer for details.

## Feed fan-out: on write or on read?

**Fan-out on write (push):** when Alice posts, write the post id into each follower's feed list.

```
post → for each of Alice's 300 followers: LPUSH feed:{follower} post_id
read feed → LRANGE feed:{me} 0 50      (fast: precomputed)
```

- ✅ Reading a feed is one fast lookup.
- ❌ A post by someone with 50M followers means 50M writes, and it's slow to finish.
- ❌ Wasted work for inactive followers.

**Fan-out on read (pull):** when Bob opens his feed, fetch recent posts from everyone he follows and merge.

- ✅ Posting is cheap; nothing is precomputed.
- ❌ Reading is expensive: following 500 accounts means 500 lookups and a merge, on every open.

## The hybrid (what big social feeds do)

- **Normal users** (most of them): fan-out on write.
- **Celebrities** (above some follower threshold): **don't** fan out; their posts are merged in at read time.
- Only fan out to **recently active** followers; rebuild inactive users' feeds on demand.

```
Bob's feed = precomputed list (from normal accounts)  ⊕  recent posts of the celebrities he follows
```

## Fan-out workers

Fan-out happens asynchronously:

```
POST /posts → store post → publish PostCreated → fan-out workers (partitioned by author) → feed caches
```

- The author sees their post immediately (read-your-writes from their own timeline).
- Followers see it within seconds.
- Backlog and lag metrics show when fan-out falls behind (lesson 15.3).

## Notification fan-out

Push notifications follow the same pattern with extra rules: per-user preferences, quiet hours, deduplication
("12 people liked your post" rather than 12 notifications), and rate limits per provider (APNs/FCM).

## Try it

A creator platform has users with 0–80M followers; the median is 150. Feeds must load in < 200 ms. Design the fan-out.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Hybrid. Fan-out on write for authors under ~100k followers, only to followers active in the last 30 days, into
per-user feed lists in Redis (capped at ~800 ids). For mega-creators, skip fan-out and merge their recent posts at read
time from a small "celebrity posts" cache. Fan-out runs in workers fed by a `PostCreated` topic partitioned by author;
inactive users' feeds are rebuilt on login.
</details>

## Key takeaways

- Pub/sub lets many services consume the same events independently.
- Fan-out on write makes reads fast but explodes for big accounts; fan-out on read is the reverse.
- The hybrid (push for most, pull for celebrities, active users only) is the standard answer.
- Run fan-out asynchronously and watch its lag.
