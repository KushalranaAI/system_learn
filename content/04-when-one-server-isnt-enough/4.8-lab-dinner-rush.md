# Lab: the dinner rush

A food ordering app runs on one small server. Every evening, traffic jumps from about 2,000 to 25,000 requests a
second. Scale it out, keep it within budget, and make it survive losing a server mid-rush.

> **Interactive version:** [open the Dinner rush lab](#/labs/dinner-rush).

## Step 1: The afternoon

2,000 req/s on one 2 vCPU server is about 40% busy. That's fine, but there's no redundancy at all.

## Step 2: The rush, on a budget

25,000 req/s with p99 under 100 ms and the whole system under $1,100/month (the database alone is $720).

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

- One server can't do it cheaply: a 32 vCPU box costs $640 and still leaves no redundancy.
- Several mid-size servers behind a load balancer: **4 × 4 vCPU ($320) + LB ($25)** gives ~6,250 req/s each, ~70%
  busy.
- Stateless servers: sessions in Redis or tokens, and files in object storage.
</details>

## Step 3: Lose a server mid-rush

<details>
<summary>Reference</summary>

- Turn on **health checks**, or a quarter of requests go to the dead server.
- After one dies, 3 servers carry 25,000 req/s, about 93% busy. That's slow, but up. For real headroom, run N+1 at the
  peak (5 servers) or scale up *before* dinner with scheduled scaling.
</details>

## Reflect

- What becomes the next bottleneck if traffic doubles again?
- How would scheduled scaling change the cost of this design?
