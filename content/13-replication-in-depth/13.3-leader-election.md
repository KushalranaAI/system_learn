# Leader election

When the leader dies, the remaining nodes must agree on a new one, and must **never** end up with two. That agreement
problem is called **consensus**. Raft is the most widely used algorithm for it (etcd, Consul, CockroachDB, TiKV,
Kafka's KRaft).

## Raft in one page

Each node is a **follower**, a **candidate** or the **leader**. Time is divided into numbered **terms**.

```
Follower ──(no heartbeat from a leader for a random 150–300 ms)──▶ Candidate
Candidate: term += 1, vote for myself, ask everyone for votes
           majority votes ──▶ Leader (sends heartbeats)
           sees a higher term ──▶ back to Follower
```

**Rules that make it safe:**
1. Each node votes **at most once per term**.
2. A candidate needs a **majority** (3 of 5, 2 of 3) to win.
3. A node only votes for a candidate whose log is **at least as up to date** as its own, so a new leader always has
   every committed entry.
4. Randomised timeouts make split votes rare; if one happens, a new term starts.

Two majorities of the same cluster always overlap in at least one node, so **two leaders can't both win the same
term**.

## Log replication

The leader appends each write to its log and sends it to followers. An entry is **committed** once a majority has
stored it; only then is the write acknowledged to the client. A committed entry survives any minority of failures.

## Why odd numbers

| Nodes | Majority | Failures tolerated |
|-------|----------|--------------------|
| 3 | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | 3 | 1 (no better than 3!) |
| 5 | 3 | 2 |
| 7 | 4 | 3 |

Clusters usually have 3 or 5 voting members. More voters means more latency per write (a bigger majority to wait for).

## Partitions

```
[A, B] | [C, D, E]      (old leader A is on the minority side)
```

- The majority side (C, D, E) elects a new leader in a higher term and keeps working.
- A can't commit anything (it can't reach a majority), so its clients get errors or timeouts.
- When the partition heals, A sees the higher term, steps down and discards its uncommitted entries.

This is the safety-over-availability choice: the minority side **stops** rather than diverging.

## Leases and stale leaders

An old leader might still *think* it's the leader for a moment (for example after a long pause). Systems guard reads
with **leader leases** (the leader only serves reads while its lease, renewed by a majority, is valid) or confirm
leadership with a round of heartbeats before answering.

## Try it

A 5-node Raft cluster runs across 3 zones as 2 + 2 + 1. Zone A (2 nodes, including the leader) loses connectivity.
What happens? What if the cluster were split 2 + 2 + 1 the other way, with the single node's zone failing?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Losing zone A leaves 3 of 5 nodes: a majority. They elect a new leader within a second or so, and writes continue.
The old leader can't commit, and steps down when it reconnects. Losing the 1-node zone leaves 4 of 5, which is still a
majority, so nothing changes. Spreading 5 nodes over 3 zones survives any single zone failure.
</details>

## Key takeaways

- Consensus lets nodes agree on one leader and one log order despite failures.
- Raft uses terms, majority votes and up-to-date logs, so two leaders can't win one term.
- Writes commit once a majority stores them.
- Use 3 or 5 voters across zones; minority partitions stop rather than diverge.
