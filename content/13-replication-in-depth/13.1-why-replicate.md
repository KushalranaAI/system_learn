# Why replicate

Replication means keeping copies of the same data on several machines. Module 5 used it as a scaling trick (read
replicas). This module looks at it in depth, because *how* copies stay in sync decides what your users can see, and
what you can lose.

## Three reasons to replicate

| Goal | How replication helps | Example |
|------|----------------------|---------|
| **Availability** | If one copy dies, another serves | Primary fails over to a replica |
| **Read scale** | Spread reads over copies | 5 replicas serve a read-heavy feed |
| **Latency** | Put a copy near users | A replica in Europe for European readers |

And one more, often forgotten: **durability**. Data acknowledged on three machines survives the loss of one disk,
host or zone.

## The central problem

Copies must agree, but the network is unreliable and messages take time (Module 12). So every replicated system answers
three questions:

1. **Who accepts writes?** One leader, several leaders, or any replica?
2. **When is a write "done"?** When the leader has it, when some replicas have it, or when all do?
3. **What does a read see?** The latest write, or possibly an older one?

The answers trade off against each other:

```
stronger agreement  ⇄  lower latency
stronger agreement  ⇄  availability during network partitions
```

## Replication is not backup

A replica faithfully copies `DROP TABLE` within milliseconds. Replication protects against **machine failure**;
backups protect against **mistakes and corruption** (lesson 5.5). You need both.

## Try it

For each, which goal (availability, read scale, latency, durability) matters most?
1. A bank's ledger database.
2. A news site's article store.
3. A game's leaderboard read by players worldwide.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. **Durability and availability**: never lose a committed transaction; survive a host failure.
2. **Read scale**: millions of readers, few writers.
3. **Latency** (and read scale): copies near players in each region; slight staleness is fine.
</details>

## Key takeaways

- Replicate for availability, read scale, latency and durability.
- Every design answers: who takes writes, when a write is done, what a read sees.
- Stronger agreement costs latency and availability.
- Replication isn't backup.
