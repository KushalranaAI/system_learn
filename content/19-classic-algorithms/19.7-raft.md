# Raft

Lesson 13.3 covered leader election. This lesson walks through Raft's full cycle, election plus log replication,
with a concrete trace and the failure cases that make it subtle.

## State each node keeps

| Persistent (survives restart) | Volatile |
|-------------------------------|----------|
| `currentTerm` | `commitIndex`: the highest entry known committed |
| `votedFor` (in this term) | `lastApplied`: the highest entry applied to the state machine |
| `log[]` of `(term, command)` | on leaders: `nextIndex[]`, `matchIndex[]` per follower |

## A trace: 5 nodes (A–E)

```
t=0     all followers, term 1. A's election timer fires first (randomised 150–300 ms).
t=150   A: term=2, votes for itself, sends RequestVote(term 2, lastLogIndex, lastLogTerm) to B–E
t=152   B, C, D grant (their logs aren't newer); E's reply is delayed
t=153   A has 4/5 votes → LEADER of term 2; sends empty AppendEntries (heartbeats)
t=200   client: SET x=1 → A appends (term 2, "x=1") at index 7
        A → AppendEntries(prevIndex 6, prevTerm 1, entries [7]) to all
t=203   B, C reply success → index 7 is on 3/5 nodes → COMMITTED → A applies it, answers the client
        followers learn commitIndex=7 on the next AppendEntries and apply it too
```

## The consistency check

Each `AppendEntries` carries the index and term of the entry **just before** the new ones. A follower rejects it if
its log doesn't match there; the leader then backs up `nextIndex` for that follower and retries, until logs match and
the follower's conflicting suffix is overwritten. This **log matching** property means that if two logs share an entry
(same index and term), everything before it is identical too.

## Failure cases

**Leader crashes after appending but before committing** (index 8 only on A and B):
- C, D or E may win the next election (their logs are "as up to date" as required: last term or index).
- If the new leader lacks index 8, it's overwritten. That's safe, because **it was never committed**, so no client
  was told it succeeded.

**A partitioned old leader:**
- A is cut off with B (a minority). C, D and E elect C in term 3.
- A still thinks it's the leader, but can't commit anything without a majority. Clients writing to A time out.
- On healing, A sees term 3, steps down, and its uncommitted entries are replaced.

**A follower restarts:** it reloads `currentTerm`, `votedFor` and its log, then catches up from the leader via the
consistency check.

## The subtle rule

A leader only counts **entries from its own term** toward commitment by majority. Earlier-term entries become
committed indirectly when a current-term entry after them commits. This prevents a known anomaly where an entry
"committed" by count could still be overwritten later.

## Snapshots and membership

- **Log compaction:** periodically snapshot the state machine and discard older log entries; send snapshots
  (`InstallSnapshot`) to followers that are far behind.
- **Membership changes** use joint consensus or single-server changes, so two disjoint majorities can never exist
  during reconfiguration.

## Try it

In a 3-node cluster (A leader, B, C), A appends entries 10 and 11 in term 4; only B receives 10; then A crashes. Can
B become leader? What happens to entries 10 and 11?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Yes: B's log (with entry 10) is at least as up to date as C's, so C will vote for B, and B wins with 2/3. Entry 10 is
on B (the new leader), so it stays and becomes committed once B commits an entry from its own term after it. Entry 11
existed only on the crashed A, so it's lost, and that's safe because it was never committed or acknowledged.
</details>

## Key takeaways

- Raft = randomised elections + majority-replicated logs + the log-matching consistency check.
- An entry is committed once a majority stores it (counting only the current term's entries directly).
- Uncommitted entries can be lost or overwritten safely; committed ones never are.
- Minority partitions can't commit; snapshots and safe membership changes complete the picture.
