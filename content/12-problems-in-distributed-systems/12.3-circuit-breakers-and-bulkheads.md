# Circuit breakers and bulkheads

When a dependency is failing, calling it harder doesn't help. Waiting on it ties up your own resources. Circuit
breakers stop the calling; bulkheads stop the damage from spreading.

## Circuit breaker

Like an electrical breaker: after too many failures it **opens**, and calls fail immediately without touching the
dependency.

```
          failures ≥ threshold
 CLOSED ───────────────────────▶ OPEN ──(after cool-down, e.g. 30 s)──▶ HALF-OPEN
   ▲  (normal calls)              (fail fast)                          (let a few trial calls through)
   └──────────── trial calls succeed ─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                   trial calls fail → back to OPEN
```

- **Closed:** calls flow normally; failures are counted (e.g. ≥50% errors over the last 20 calls).
- **Open:** fail instantly with a fallback. The dependency gets time to recover.
- **Half-open:** a few test calls; if they succeed, close the breaker.

## Fallbacks

What does the caller do when the breaker is open?

| Dependency | Fallback |
|------------|----------|
| Recommendations | Show bestsellers (static or cached) |
| Reviews | Hide the reviews section |
| Pricing | **No safe fallback**: fail the request clearly |
| Feature flags | Last known values |

Decide fallbacks per dependency, ahead of time. "Degrade, don't die" for non-essential features; fail fast and clearly
for essential ones.

## Bulkheads

A ship's bulkheads keep one flooded compartment from sinking the ship. In software: **separate resource pools per
dependency**, so one slow dependency can't consume everything.

```
❌ one shared pool of 200 threads/connections
   reviews becomes slow → all 200 threads wait on reviews → checkout can't get a thread

✅ bulkheads
   checkout: 100 · reviews: 30 · recommendations: 30 · other: 40
   reviews becomes slow → only its 30 are stuck; checkout is untouched
```

Bulkheads apply at many levels:
- connection pools and concurrency limits per downstream service;
- separate worker queues per job type (lesson 7.2);
- separate clusters or cells for different customers (big tenants isolated from small ones).

## Load shedding

When **you** are the overloaded service, reject excess work early and cheaply (503 + `Retry-After`) instead of
accepting everything and timing out. Prioritise: shed recommendations before checkout.

## Try it

The product page calls inventory, reviews and recommendations. Reviews starts taking 10 s per call, and the product
page now fails entirely. Which two patterns fix this, and how?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Bulkheads:** give reviews its own small concurrency limit or pool, so slow review calls can't exhaust the threads
the page needs for inventory. **Circuit breaker + fallback** on reviews: once failures and timeouts cross the
threshold, stop calling it and render the page without reviews. Plus a short timeout on the reviews call (lesson
12.2).
</details>

## Key takeaways

- Circuit breakers stop calls to failing dependencies and let them recover.
- Plan fallbacks per dependency: degrade non-essential features, fail essential ones clearly.
- Bulkheads isolate resources so one slow dependency can't take everything down.
- Shed load early when you're the one overloaded.
